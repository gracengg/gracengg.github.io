SQL / Revenue Diagnostics / Analytical Extension

Retail Revenue Growth & Data Quality

Why did revenue rise only modestly when transaction volume increased? I used SQL to reconcile category-level growth and tested whether the customer identifiers could support a credible retention analysis.

Author
Grace Ngo
Data
2,000 source rows, 2022–2023
Implementation
SQLite queries, Python standard-library runner and seven tests

The business question

Overall growth can hide opposing movements. I separated transaction volume from average transaction value, reconciled category contributions and examined how much revenue depended on larger purchases. I also tested the identity fields before treating repeat customer IDs as evidence of retention.

The result

Revenue rose from 452,825 to 458,895 source currency units, a 1.34% increase. Transaction count rose 4.61%, while average transaction value fell from 463.96 to 449.46. Clothing and Electronics gains were partly offset by Beauty’s decline.

The SQL growth bridge uses a symmetric volume/value decomposition that reconciles to each category’s revenue change. At the illustrative 1,000-unit transaction cutoff, 20.3% of 2023 transactions accounted for 63.8% of revenue.

A data defect that changes the conclusion

Of 155 customer IDs, 151 map to more than one recorded gender value, and all 155 have inconsistent age/gender profiles. The same identifiers therefore cannot support a defensible retention or demographic-targeting claim. The project reports this defect rather than presenting a misleading customer segmentation.

For revenue analysis, 1,997 transactions remain eligible. Three rows lack revenue inputs. Ten valid transactions with missing age are retained because age is not required to calculate sales. All source rows remain in raw staging.

What I would investigate next

I would first examine Beauty’s lower purchase value and reduced transaction count, then test whether Clothing’s transaction growth depends on promotions or lower-priced products. That requires SKU, promotion, return and cost definitions that this dataset does not provide. Customer retention would require reconciliation to a trusted identity source first.

Technical delivery

The repository includes documented SQLite queries with CTEs, window functions and denominator-aware shares; integer monetary storage; a reproducible loader; generated result tables; and seven tests. Independent source totals and the category bridge are checked before results are written. GitHub Actions repeats the tests and complete run.

Data scope

The supplied dataset’s upstream creator, currency and sampling method are not documented. The source does not establish a real retailer’s performance, and the COGS field has no clear unit basis. This project makes no profit, retention, sales-uplift or retailer-wide seasonality claim. Its contribution is the added analytical question, executable growth reconciliation and explicit data-quality decisions.
