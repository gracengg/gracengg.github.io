CCLA Engagement & Program Reporting

I built a multi-page Power BI report to bring community-engagement participation, academic profiles and access indicators into a connected reporting workflow.

Tools

Who CCLA serves

Dickinson College’s Center for Civic Learning & Action (CCLA) connects civic learning with community engagement. It works with students, faculty, staff and community partners to develop civic leadership and support communities.

The retained report covers six engagement categories: Bonner Program, Montgomery Service Leaders (MSL), Community Engagement Fellows (CEF), CCLA student employment, engagement recognition and service trips. One student can appear in several categories, so program memberships and unique students answer different questions.

Organization context: Dickinson’s CCLA overview and mission and approach.

The reporting question

Who do CCLA programs reach, and how can staff compare participation alongside academic and cohort context?

The source records store several programs, majors and minors across columns. A usable report needs to preserve those overlapping relationships, keep student counts distinct from activity counts, and make the comparison group clear when a reader changes a filter.

What I built

Prepared the source

Used Power Query to remove blank rows, assign data types, standardize selected labels and derive academic-pathway fields.

Reshaped the relationships

Unpivoted major, minor and program fields into separate tables so multiple qualifications and memberships could be analyzed.

Designed the report

Built overview, academic, access and program-detail views with DAX summaries and slicers for cohort, major and recorded access indicators.

The measurement choices behind a credible report

In reviewing the retained copy, I focused on three definitions that determine whether the report can be interpreted correctly.

Count students separately from memberships

A student can participate in several programs. Student-level indicators need a unique-person denominator; program reach needs a distinct count within each selected program. Adding the program bars does not produce the size of the student population.

Show the proportion and its coverage

A participation proportion should count affirmative responses rather than every populated flag. Where some records are unknown, the known-response denominator and the share of records covered belong alongside the percentage.

Keep status descriptions separate from program impact

Recorded graduate, current-student and withdrawal statuses describe the retained snapshot. A program-performance claim would require comparable cohorts and time-defined outcomes. Here, the report supports descriptive program review.
